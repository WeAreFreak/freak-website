What's happening now

At Freak we have seen material changes to ecommerce numbers, including revenue, as large language models such as ChatGPT and Amazon's own assistant begin to replace search and paid clicks. They have already taken a real slice of the two biggest digital channels most brands have, across Amazon, Google and brand sites.

The way shoppers find products is changing. A shopper asks a question in plain language, and instead of pages of listings the assistant offers a handful of highly relevant products, with comparisons and a summary of what reviewers say.

Many brands still haven't connected this to what they're seeing in their numbers. That's why we wrote this.

From Rufus to Alexa for Shopping

Amazon launched its first shopping assistant, Rufus, in 2024. On 13th May 2026 it replaced Rufus with Alexa for Shopping, starting in the US. The new assistant brings together Rufus's product knowledge and Amazon shopping history with the personal context of Alexa+, and Amazon says Rufus had already helped more than 300 million customers in 2025.

The biggest change is where it lives. Shoppers can now ask their question in the main Amazon search bar, on the app and the website, so the assistant sits exactly where most shopping journeys begin. It compares products, shows up to a year of price history and can build a basket. A beta has since launched in the UAE, and we expect more marketplaces to follow.

The name has changed. Everything we learned from Rufus about how these assistants choose products still applies, and moving the assistant into the search bar makes it matter more.

The good news

Our data shows that brands with a strong foundation are better placed to be favoured by Amazon's assistant. That means complete product information, a body of positive reviews and clear brand messaging, because the assistant learns from exactly that data.

It makes sense. A language model takes a huge amount of information and refines it down to an answer for one question. Bigger brands simply contribute more data for the model to learn from, and that gives them an advantage. These models are designed not to give the same answer every time, but in our user journey testing on Amazon, the range of products bought through the assistant has narrowed sharply.

The bad news

AI curation means fewer products make the shortlist, and being one of the few the assistant picks is becoming critical. A brand that ranks first for a key search term can now be fifth in the assistant's answer, because of the randomness built into these models.

For bigger brands there is another consequence. Some of our clients hold 80% of the top ten listings for key search terms. In an assistant's answer they might get one of five places, which is 20% of that digital storefront. And where they used to hold the top spot, they can now be anywhere from first to fifth.

Let's be clear. If every Amazon sale went through the assistant today, it would be a volcanic reshaping of the market. With the assistant now in the search bar, that's no longer a hypothetical.

What brands should do now

AI shopping assistants call for a shift in how brands approach Amazon. These are the five things we focus on with our clients.

1. Write for conversation, not just keywords

Product pages need to speak the language of conversational AI. That means answering the questions shoppers actually ask, in the bullets and the A+ content, and weaving the brand story and unique selling points in a way the assistant can understand. If your yoga mat is made from recycled rubber, say so in words that match a question like "eco friendly yoga mats".

2. Treat reviews as inputs

Positive, detailed reviews feed straight into the AI. Encourage genuine reviews and make sure reviews that break Amazon's guidelines are reported, because the assistant uses that data to judge whether a product suits a shopper and can be trusted.

3. Give the AI complete data

AI thrives on complete, accurate information. Fill in every backend attribute and use A+ content for both the brand story and the detailed specifications an assistant needs to recommend you with confidence.

4. Keep the foundations strong

Organic rank and a positive sales history still matter. They build the authority that makes a product more likely to be recognised and recommended, and well planned advertising helps hold that visibility while assistants mature.

5. Keep testing and adapting

Amazon's assistant has already changed its name and its place on the page in two years, and it will keep changing. We monitor how it behaves in each category and market, test content and strategy and adapt quickly so our clients stay visible and favoured.

In short
  • Alexa for Shopping replaced Rufus in May 2026 and sits in the search bar
  • AI assistants answer with a few products, not pages of results
  • Strong, complete, well reviewed listings are favoured
  • Write for questions, complete every attribute and look after reviews
