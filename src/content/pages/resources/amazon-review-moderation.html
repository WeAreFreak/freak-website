Why four stars matters

Star ratings are one of the first things a shopper sees, in search, on the product page and now in AI shopping assistants. Products at four stars and above win more clicks and more sales, and a dip below four can cost a range far more than the reviews themselves suggest.

Early reviews matter most. When a new product has only a handful, one unfair review can drag the rating down at exactly the moment the launch needs momentum. On one Dr Scholl's launch, a single early review could have had a serious impact, so we moderated it straight away.

What breaks the rules

A negative review isn't a reason to report it. Honest feedback, good or bad, is what makes reviews useful, and brands should answer it, not try to bury it. But many low ratings aren't really reviews of the product at all. Amazon's Community Guidelines exclude reviews that are about things like these.

  • Delivery, packaging or courier problems that the product didn't cause
  • Seller service or questions, which belong in seller feedback
  • A different product, or using it for something it isn't for
  • Promotional content, links or competitor plugs
  • Repeated or coordinated reviews that point to bots or a campaign

Amazon explains how reviews work for shoppers in its guide to writing and finding reviews. Our moderation framework turns the full guidelines, and industry review standards, into codes we can apply consistently.

Our six step process

  1. Download. Every review for the range, with its rating, date and text, using review research tools.
  2. Classify. Each review under three stars is checked by AI against our moderation framework.
  3. Code. Reviews that break the guidelines get a moderation code that says why.
  4. Report. We draft the report for each claim and submit it through Amazon's review tools, then follow up on the cases.
  5. Escalate. Patterns that suggest bots or competitor activity go to the brand's Amazon account manager.
  6. Prioritise. We rank ASINs where moderation would make the biggest difference, either low rated products that could reach four stars or high volume products where every tenth of a star counts.

Why it runs on 20 years of knowledge

The AI does the reading. The judgement comes from people who have run Amazon accounts for a long time and know how Amazon treats each type of claim. That knowledge sits in the framework, in the codes and in the way reports are written, which is why the tool gets better results than a general model asked to find bad reviews.

In short
  • Four stars and above wins clicks, sales and AI recommendations
  • Report reviews that break the guidelines, answer the ones that don't
  • Check every review under three stars, consistently
  • Prioritise the ASINs where a lift in rating is worth the most
