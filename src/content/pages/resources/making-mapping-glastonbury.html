The brief

The V&A holds a growing Glastonbury Festival archive. Posters, photographs, programmes, interviews and recordings, plus memories sent in by festival goers through an open call. The museum wanted all of it to be explored by anyone, anywhere, and to feel as alive as the festival itself.

It also had to work for two very different audiences. Casual visitors who want to browse and stumble across things, and researchers, from academics to journalists and historians, who need a tool they can rely on.

Five maps, five decades

We designed Mapping Glastonbury around the place. Five 3D maps show the festival site at each major stage of its history, from the 1970s to today, each built from satellite and mapping data and from historical maps and photographs of the site.

Visitors fly over the fields, move through the years and open what's pinned to the landscape. Memories, visions, sounds, spaces, experiences and locations, each linked to items in the archive. The maps are the interface for more than 500 pieces of content, and more are being added.

Built for the browser, and for phones

The biggest decision was where it would run. An app download would have made the 3D easier and the audience smaller. We built a progressive web app instead, with Three.js and the Nuxt framework, so it opens in any modern browser.

That pushed the limits of what was possible at the time, particularly on mobile, where every model, texture and sound had to load quickly and run smoothly on ordinary phones. It was, as we wrote for the V&A, relatively uncharted territory.

  • Three.js for the 3D maps and the camera that flies between them
  • Nuxt for the app, the routing and the content
  • A content model built to scale, so the V&A can keep adding to the archive
  • Mobile first optimisation, so it runs on the phones people actually carry

Respecting a cultural institution

Glastonbury means a lot to a lot of people. The design had to be playful enough to explore and serious enough to trust, balancing entertainment and information without letting either win.

Launch and after

Mapping Glastonbury launched in June 2021, alongside the V&A's three day Glastonbury Weekender, and Creative Boom covered the launch. Later that year we wrote up the project for the V&A's own blog. The app is still live on the V&A website, and it sits within the museum's wider Glastonbury research.

In short
  • Five 3D maps of the festival site, 1970s to today
  • More than 500 archive items, with more being added
  • A progressive web app built with Three.js and Nuxt
  • Designed for casual visitors and researchers alike
